# Historical Manuscript Skeuomorphism

## Signature
aged paper textures, leather or wood framing, wax seals, ribbons, page tabs,
ornamental dividers
metal corner details, ruled tables, ink-like typography, worn edges

## Suited for
historical games, educational experiences, museum interfaces, fantasy
worldbuilding
simulation dashboards
narrative menus, culture-focused websites

## Layout
interface designed like a physical page, ledger, or bound book; framed
panels, generous margins
formal alignment
tabbed navigation, compartmentalized sections that feel attached, printed,
pinned
or layered onto a surface

## Colors
- muted material-inspired tones drawn from paper, ink, leather, wood, and metal
- gentle contrast and naturally weathered value shifts rather than crisp
  synthetic intensity
- token mapping: `primary` = oxblood or archival ink
  `secondary` = tarnished brass or muted leather, `base-100` = pale parchment
  `base-200` = aged paper, `base-300` = tea-stained paper
- `--noise: 1`, `--depth: 1`

## Imagery
printed, sketched, engraved, stamped, or mounted-on-paper imagery with subtle
surface wear
soft depth
and a consistent physical-material treatment

## Density
medium to high, organized into clearly bounded sheets, labels, columns
and tables so the interface stays orderly and readable

## Motion
restrained motion mimicking physical interaction: page turning, sliding
drawers, unfolding notes
stamped feedback
slight object lift; no flashy digital effects

## Shape
rectangular sheets, plaques, labels, medallions, tabs, seals, decorative
flourishes
slightly imperfect edges
layered borders, tangible object-like construction; `--radius-*` between
0.25rem and 1rem

## Typography
elegant serif and calligraphic typography with strong hierarchy
historical display face for headings
readable old-style serif for body; type treated as printed or written in ink
Suggested: Cormorant Garamond, Cinzel, EB Garamond, IM Fell English, Alegreya

## Origins
medieval manuscript precedents; contemporary digital adaptation; European
and Mediterranean manuscript workshops
translates parchment, illumination, rubrication, seals,
and book structure from hand-produced medieval manuscripts into screen surfaces
its digital mechanism belongs to the newer skeuomorphic practice
of using familiar physical cues to frame interaction
