# Claymorphism

## Signature
inflated components, soft three-dimensional shading, pastel palette,
oversized radii
chunky icons, toy-like depth

## Suited for
education, onboarding, creative tools, playful consumer products

## Layout
simple centered scenes built from a few chunky modules; room around each object
floating clusters rather than tight dashboard grids

## Colors
- two or three soft pastel families with warm shaded sides and dark readable text
- brightest clay color reserved for the active object
- token mapping: `primary` = brightest peach, lilac, or sky clay hue
  `secondary` = complementary soft pastel, `base-100`/`base-200`/`base-300` = creamy
  gently tinted, progressively shaded surfaces
- `--depth: 1`

## Imagery
modeled clay characters, soft three-dimensional icons, toy-like objects with
matte surfaces
and consistent studio lighting

## Density
low to medium; one friendly task or lesson per cluster with short supporting
copy
`*-lg` size variants reinforce the chunky, informal scale

## Motion
buoyant lifts, soft squash and stretch, gentle bounce on success; repeated
motion kept slow
and sparse

## Shape
inflated blobs, thick capsules, rounded cubes, oversized radii with soft side
shading instead
of sharp borders
`--radius-*` between 1rem and 2rem

## Typography
rounded, heavy display lettering paired with a simpler friendly sans-serif
headings kept short so their playful mass does not crowd controls
Suggested: Fredoka, Baloo 2, Nunito, M PLUS Rounded 1c, Sniglet

## Origins
around 2021–early 2020s; online UI and 3D illustration communities;
contemporary interface label
not a historic art movement; designers combined rounded 3D illustration,
toy-like volume
and soft skeuomorphic lighting after a decade of flat UI; commonly attributed
to Michał Malewicz
though its distributed visual development makes a single origin hard to prove
