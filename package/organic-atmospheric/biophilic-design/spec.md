# Biophilic Design

## Signature
botanical imagery, organic shapes, earthy palette, natural textures, daylight
gradients
gentle motion

## Suited for
wellness, sustainability, hospitality, healthcare, lifestyle products

## Layout
open breathing flow inspired by paths and clearings
alternate broad natural imagery with calm content zones and soft section
transitions

## Colors
- primary: leaf or moss green
- secondary: clay or sky blue
- base-100: warm mineral white
- base-200: pale sand
- base-300: soft stone
- texture: `--noise: 1`

## Imagery
authentic plants, landscapes, daylight interiors, material close-ups, human
activity in nature
no generic leaf decoration

## Density
low to medium; generous pauses, concise groups, open space for a restorative
tone

## Motion
gentle breeze, growth, water, sunlight, breathing-inspired movement at slow
amplitude
stop ambient effects around focused tasks

## Shape
leaf arcs, pebble forms, branch-like dividers, soft asymmetry, natural
material edges
controls not turned into novelty objects; `--radius-*` between 0.75rem and
1.5rem

## Typography
humanist sans-serif or organic serif with open forms; relaxed leading
sentence-case headings that feel calm rather than clinical
Suggested: Fraunces, Lora, Alegreya, Nunito Sans, Cormorant Infant

## Origins
Conceptual roots in the 1960s–1980s; design field formalized in the 2000s
Origin: environmental psychology, biology, architecture, and interior design
Erich Fromm used "biophilia" in the 1960s
E. O. Wilson popularized the hypothesis in his 1984 book Biophilia
Stephen Kellert and collaborators developed biophilic design as a framework
connecting built
environments with nature; digital interfaces borrow its imagery and patterns
but cannot reproduce
all the spatial and sensory effects studied in architecture
Sources: biophilicdesign.umn.edu, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8334556/
