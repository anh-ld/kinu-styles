# Wabi-Sabi

## Signature
asymmetry, weathered texture, muted earth tones, irregular edges, visible
imperfection
quiet composition

## Suited for
craft, hospitality, wellness, food, slow-living brands, artisan commerce

## Layout
quiet asymmetrical arrangements; irregular margins, visible pauses
small material details that reward close attention rather than perfect alignment

## Colors
- primary: weathered clay or moss
- secondary: oxidized metal or tea
- base-100: linen
- base-200: tea-stained beige
- base-300: soft ash
- subtle unevenness; no polished high-saturation accent
- texture: `--noise: 1`

## Imagery
macro photography of handmade objects, aged wood, ceramics, fabric, food
weathered surfaces that reveal imperfection and use

## Density
low; one crafted object or short passage occupies a generous field without
explanatory clutter

## Motion
slow imperfect crossfades, hand-paced reveals, subtle material changes
nothing looping with machine-like precision

## Shape
irregular edges, incomplete circles, uneven vessels, torn fibers,
asymmetrical frames
imperfections remain controlled; `--radius-*` set to 0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
quiet serif, Mincho, or humanist face; relaxed leading; modest scale
no glossy display lettering or mechanically tight spacing
Suggested: Shippori Mincho, Zen Old Mincho, Sawarabi Mincho, Yuji Syuku, Noto
Serif JP

## Origins
Medieval precedents; deeply associated with tea culture from the 15th–16th
centuries
Origin: Japan
wabi and sabi accumulated meanings over centuries, connected with
Zen-influenced tea practice
tea masters including Sen no Rikyū shaped the 16th-century tradition
modern commercial "wabi-sabi" often simplifies a complex aesthetic
and ethical history into a palette of beige ceramics
Sources: metmuseum.org/ja/essays/the-japanese-tea-ceremony
