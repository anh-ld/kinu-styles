# Solarpunk

## Signature
lush vegetation, renewable technology, warm daylight, community
infrastructure, organic architecture
optimistic illustration

## Suited for
climate products, sustainability platforms, community tools, speculative
projects

## Layout
connected community ecosystem; weave buildings, gardens, energy, transit
and shared resources through open sunlit sections

## Colors
- primary: leaf or algae green
- secondary: solar blue or terracotta
- base-100: warm white
- base-200: pale concrete
- base-300: light soil-gray
- bright daylight, not dystopian neon

## Imagery
lush inhabited cities, renewable infrastructure, cooperative work, repair,
food systems
biodiversity
technology integrated with landscape

## Density
medium; meaningful systems detail in clear diagrams; preserve an optimistic
sense of air, light
and public space

## Motion
trace energy and water flows, grow planted elements, shift sunlight
animate community activity with gentle cyclical motion

## Shape
organic canopies, terraces, greenhouse curves, branching networks
clean photovoltaic geometry in integrated structures; `--radius-*` between
0.75rem and 1.5rem

## Typography
optimistic humanist sans-serif with warm editorial accents; plain language
technical sustainability data clearly labeled
Suggested: Fraunces, Alegreya Sans, Sora, Raleway, Exo 2

## Origins
Term introduced in 2008; community growth in the 2010s
Origin: online speculative-fiction and environmental communities
future-facing movement centered on ecological repair, renewable technology,
mutual aid
and equitable social systems; word traced to a 2008 blog post "From Steampunk
to Solarpunk"
Tumblr discussion
fiction, art, and manifestos expanded the community especially around 2014
not merely a palette of leaves and solar panels
Sources: mdpi.com/2409-9287/8/4/73, solarpunkpress.net
