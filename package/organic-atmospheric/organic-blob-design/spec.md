# Organic / Blob Design

## Signature
blob shapes, asymmetric curves, fluid containers, soft edges, freeform
layout, playful motion

## Suited for
wellness, creative tools, lifestyle brands, onboarding, approachable
technology products

## Layout
loose flowing path with asymmetrical overlaps; one dependable reading spine
for text and controls

## Colors
- primary: strongest botanical or fruit hue
- secondary: soft neighboring mineral tone
- base-100: cream
- base-200: pale mineral
- base-300: gently tinted earth
- blended transitions that follow the edges of organic forms

## Imagery
cutout people, plants, hand-modeled objects
abstract fluid fields whose contours interact with nearby content

## Density
low to medium; generous internal space in curved containers
no long text inside narrow blob extremities
`*-lg` daisyUI size variants for chunky informal scale

## Motion
morph silhouettes slowly, float small accents
sections ease into one another with soft organic timing rather than rigid slides

## Shape
asymmetric blobs, pebble cards, soft waves, irregular circular crops
interactive targets kept predictably sized
`--radius-*` between 1rem and 2rem

## Typography
warm humanist sans-serif or soft display serif; generous leading
keep type on calm regions instead of wrapping it around every curve
Suggested: Fraunces, Gabarito, Varela Round, Quicksand, Urbanist

## Origins
Early 20th-century biomorphic art; digital revival in the late 2010s
Origin: modern art and contemporary vector-based product design
early-20th-century artists used abstract forms suggesting plants, bodies, and
cells
vector tools and flexible web layouts made irregular silhouettes deployable
at scale
producing the late-2010s wave of soft brand blobs
Sources: tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/b/biomorphic
