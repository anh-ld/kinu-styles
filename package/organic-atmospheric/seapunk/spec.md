# Seapunk

## Signature
turquoise water, dolphins and shells, early CGI, chrome objects, tropical
gradients
aquatic surrealism

## Suited for
music, fashion, youth culture, events, experimental commerce, nostalgic
campaigns

## Layout
float content, chrome objects, and aquatic motifs through layered water-like
space
wave bands and loose collage rather than terrestrial grids

## Colors
- primary: turquoise or aqua
- secondary: cobalt or purple
- base-100: pale seafoam
- base-200: light aqua
- base-300: watery blue-gray
- tropical gradients and bright watery highlights
- texture: `--depth: 1`

## Imagery
dolphins, shells, waves, tropical plants, early CGI creatures, reflective
objects
surreal ocean-photo composites

## Density
medium to high visual density with concise copy
essential commerce or event information on opaque aquatic panels

## Motion
bob objects, ripple surfaces, rotate early-CGI forms, trail bubbles
shift underwater light with playful internet-era timing

## Shape
bubbles, waves, shells, chrome rings, splash contours, fins, soft
early-three-dimensional primitives
`--radius-*` between 0.75rem and 1.5rem

## Typography
playful rounded or digital display type with a simple sans-serif body
small text dark and crisp against bright water imagery
Suggested: Fascinate, Monoton, Righteous, Bungee Shade, Pixelify Sans

## Origins
2011–mid-2010s
Origin: Tumblr, Twitter, and online electronic-music networks
began as both an internet joke and a small music-and-image scene
DJ Lil Internet coined the word in a 2011 tweet
turquoise oceans, dolphins, shells, 3D grids, Windows-era graphics
and aquatic fashion circulated rapidly through Tumblr
and music communities before being absorbed into mainstream pop imagery
Sources: knowyourmeme.com/memes/cultures/seapunk, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seapunk
