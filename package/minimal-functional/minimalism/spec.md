# Minimalism

## Signature
dominant negative space, very few elements, restrained palette, single focal
point
plain typography, minimal ornament

## Suited for
focused tools, landing pages, portfolios, premium commerce, reading experiences
single-purpose products

## Layout
center each view on one task or message, eliminate secondary containers
use large uninterrupted areas to isolate the primary action

## Colors
- primary: single restrained accent
- secondary: low-chroma tonal neighbor
- base-100/base-200/base-300: tight white-to-gray or near-black tonal range

## Imagery
one purposeful image or none, quiet setting
exclude any visual that does not strengthen the central message or decision

## Density
intentionally low, shorten visible choices
defer supporting detail until the user requests or needs it

## Motion
only to explain state or continuity; short fades and direct transitions, no
ambient decorative loops

## Shape
few containers, plain rectangles, modest radii, unembellished icons
only the borders or dividers needed to clarify interaction; `--radius-*`
between 0.25rem and 1rem

## Typography
one neutral family, sparse type scale, concise copy, generous line spacing
hierarchy through size and weight alone
Suggested: Inter, Manrope, Instrument Sans, DM Sans, Source Sans 3

## Origins
1960s art, recurring in digital design since the 1990s; New York art with
wider modernist precedents
— literal form, industrial fabrication, repetition, reduced composition
a sparse interface is an adaptation of that art movement rather than the
movement itself
Sources: moma.org/collection/terms/minimalism
