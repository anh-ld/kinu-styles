# Swiss Style

## Signature
modular grid, asymmetric layout, grotesque sans-serif, generous whitespace,
objective hierarchy
flush-left alignment

## Suited for
editorial products, documentation, portfolios, wayfinding, information-heavy
interfaces

## Layout
mathematically consistent modular grid, text and image placed asymmetrically
across columns
every element aligned to shared vertical and horizontal anchors

## Colors
- primary: single signal color — signal red, cobalt, or yellow
- secondary: neutral gray or black
- base-100: white
- base-200: very light gray
- base-300: firmer grid gray

## Imagery
documentary photography, diagrams, objective product images
decisive cropping to grid boundaries, no ornamental framing

## Density
medium to high information density while protecting large intentional voids
that clarify section
changes and priority

## Motion
linear grid-aligned wipes, precise slides, immediate state changes; no
elastic or ornamental motion

## Shape
rules, bars, circles, rectangles as functional alignment or labeling devices;
corners sharp
geometry exact
`--radius-*` set to 0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
grotesque sans-serif on a strict scale, flush-left with ragged right edge;
weight, size
and spacing express an objective hierarchy
Suggested: Archivo, Inter, Work Sans, Libre Franklin, IBM Plex Sans

## Origins
1950s–1960s; Zurich and Basel, Switzerland
the International Typographic Style took recognizable form in 1950s Switzerland
and spread internationally in the 1960s — asymmetric grids, sans-serif
typography, photography
objective hierarchy; Helvetica and Univers emblematic though the movement was
broader
