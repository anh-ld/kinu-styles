# Japanese Minimalism

## Signature
meaningful whitespace, precise spacing, neutral palette, fine rules, natural
materials
restrained typography

## Suited for
wellness, hospitality, architecture, productivity, premium commerce,
contemplative products

## Layout
use ma — the purposeful interval between elements — with asymmetrical balance
precise modular spacing
and a quiet sequence of thresholds between content regions

## Colors
- primary: indigo or ink
- secondary: muted cedar or moss
- base-100: rice paper
- base-200: warm linen
- base-300: pale stone

## Imagery
restrained architecture, gardens, craft, food, fabric, material close-ups
with quiet light
careful cropping
few subjects

## Density
low, one object, thought, or action at a time; emptiness treated as an active
compositional element

## Motion
slow screen-like slides, gentle reveals, precise fades with clear pauses;
deliberate and finite

## Shape
thin rules, exact rectangles, circles, screens, occasional brush or paper edges
avoid an excess of soft Western-style cards; `--radius-*` set to 0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
restrained Gothic or Mincho families with careful Japanese spacing, balanced
Latin companions
optional vertical type only where reading order stays clear
Suggested: Noto Sans JP, Noto Serif JP, Zen Kaku Gothic New, Zen Old Mincho,
Shippori Mincho

## Origins
long cultural precedents, global design label especially visible from the
late 20th century; Japan
an external umbrella rather than one historical movement — ma, emptiness,
asymmetry
material honesty
Zen practice, Shinto relationships with nature, modern Japanese design
collapsing those histories into a generic "Zen look" erases important
differences
