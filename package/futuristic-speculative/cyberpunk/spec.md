# Cyberpunk

## Signature
glitch effects, scanlines, RGB separation, flicker, distortion, digital
noise; `--noise: 1`
`--depth: 1`

## Suited for
games, security tools, crypto products, nightlife, science-fiction experiences

## Layout
dense urban telemetry, feeds, and navigation layered around one protected
core task
using stacked panes and off-axis overlays without hiding essential controls

## Colors
- primary: neon cyan
- secondary: magenta or acid green
- base-100 → base-300: dirty blue-gray, near-black, black
- near-black and dirty blue-gray base, with neon cyan, magenta, acid green,
  and warning red as sharp electrical signals

## Imagery
rain-lit megacities, surveillance feeds, augmented faces, circuitry, street
signage
industrial alleys
corrupted corporate media

## Density
high information and visual density with bright priority channels
so users can separate actionable data from atmospheric noise
`*-sm` size variants preserve this compact working density

## Motion
brief RGB splits, scanline sweeps, terminal updates, feed glitches
rapid pane locks tied to loading, intrusion
or system events

## Shape
clipped panels, angular brackets, cable paths, segmented bars, inset terminals
layered translucent plates with sharp corners; `--radius-*` between 0 and
0.25rem

## Typography
condensed technical sans-serif paired with monospace data text
glitch or kana-like display accents only where they do not imitate meaningful
content
Suggested: Orbitron, Rajdhani, Share Tech Mono, Oxanium, Chakra Petch

## Origins
early 1980s–present; Anglophone science fiction, with major visual
development in American
and Japanese media
Bruce Bethke wrote the story "Cyberpunk" in 1980 and published it in 1983
supplying the term used later for a wider movement
William Gibson's 1984 novel Neuromancer became a defining text
while films, manga, anime, and games established the visual contrast of high
technology
corporate power
dense cities, and precarious street life
