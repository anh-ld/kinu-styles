# Afrofuturism

## Signature
cosmic imagery, diasporic pattern, metallic accents, bold geometry,
speculative technology
ancestral symbolism

## Suited for
culture, music, fashion, education, entertainment, visionary technology
campaigns

## Layout
monumental radial or processional compositions
combining strong central figures with layered pattern fields
cosmic space, and clear narrative progression

## Colors
- primary: context-appropriate royal purple or indigo
- secondary: gold or copper
- base-100 → base-300: dark earth, near-black, deep black
- deep black, indigo, royal purple, earth, gold, copper
  electric accents informed by the specific African or diasporic context

## Imagery
culturally grounded textiles, architecture, adornment, portraiture, ancestral
symbols, landscapes
and speculative machines — without treating traditions as interchangeable

## Density
medium to high symbolic density; each motif supported with enough context
practical information kept in clearly structured regions

## Motion
unfold patterns, orbit cosmic elements, activate metallic circuitry
transition between ancestral and future imagery with ceremonial pacing

## Shape
geometry drawn from the chosen culture's pattern, craft, architecture, and
symbolism
combined with stars, rings
shields, and engineered forms; `--radius-*` between 0.25rem and 1rem

## Typography
bold contemporary display type that respects the cultural reference
paired with a highly readable sans-serif
avoid invented pseudo-African lettering
Suggested: Afacad Flux, Ubuntu, Syne, Unbounded, Space Grotesk

## Origins
long artistic lineage; term coined in 1993; African diasporic literature,
music, visual art
scholarship
and technology culture; cultural critic Mark Dery coined "Afrofuturism" in 1993
but the work it names is much older — W. E. B. Du Bois, Sun Ra,
and many later artists used speculative futures
technology, African histories, and diasporic experience to examine liberation
and identity
the field cannot be reduced to a decorative sci-fi motif set
Sources: nmaahc.si.edu (Afrofuturism explained), smithsonianmag.com (What is
Afrofuturism?)
