# Cassette Futurism

## Signature
CRT displays, chunky switches, industrial panels, amber readouts, physical
labels
analog instrumentation; `--depth: 1`

## Suited for
games, developer tools, media players, science-fiction products, immersive
entertainment

## Layout
a heavy modular console with a dominant CRT, adjacent hardware bays, physical
status strips
and labels attached directly to controls

## Colors
- primary: amber or green phosphor
- secondary: military olive
- base-100 → base-300: charcoal, near-black metal, black
- charcoal, military olive, beige plastic, worn metal with amber or green
  phosphor displays
  and small red warning indicators

## Imagery
chunky switches, tape decks, CRT readouts, cables, vents, industrial labels
late-century spacecraft or computer hardware

## Density
high density through clearly separated physical panels, engraved legends
and dedicated readout zones rather than floating digital cards
`*-sm` size variants preserve this compact working density

## Motion
CRT boot sequences, scan refresh, tape motion, switch snaps, relay delays
occasional signal dropout with believable hardware response

## Shape
boxy housings, vents, recessed screens, chunky toggles, rotary knobs,
handles, screws
thick molded bezels
`--radius-*` between 0.25rem and 1rem

## Typography
monospaced phosphor text for displays; condensed industrial lettering for
panel labels
terminology terse and mechanically aligned
Suggested: Share Tech Mono, Chakra Petch, Oxanium, IBM Plex Mono, VT323

## Origins
source period from the mid-1970s–late 1980s; named retrospectively online;
industrial technology
analog media
film, and television; a retrospective internet label for futures imagined
with tape decks, CRTs
membrane keys
switches, and bulky modular hardware; name recorded as a later coinage on TV
Tropes
— original production designers did not generally use it for their own work
