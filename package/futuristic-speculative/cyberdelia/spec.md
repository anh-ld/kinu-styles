# Cyberdelia

## Signature
fractals, acid color, kaleidoscopic imagery, early three-dimensional type,
rave graphics
cybernetic motifs
`--depth: 1`

## Suited for
music, nightlife, games, experimental technology, events, retro-internet
campaigns

## Layout
a portal-like sequence of fractal fields, early-three-dimensional objects,
and rave navigation
anchoring each scene around one central entry point

## Colors
- primary: acid green or ultraviolet
- secondary: cyan or hot orange
- base-100 → base-300: deep indigo, dark violet, black
- acid green, ultraviolet, cyan, hot orange, black,
  and spectral gradients in luminous combinations that resemble projected club graphics

## Imagery
fractals, kaleidoscopes, chrome insects, cybernetic eyes, wireframes, early
CGI, rave footage
psychedelic computer diagrams

## Density
high visual density but low to medium copy
event or product information kept on stable overlays above the shifting
cyber-psychedelic field

## Motion
travel through kaleidoscopic tunnels, iterate fractals, rotate cybernetic forms
pulse patterns to rhythm without unsafe flashing

## Shape
spirals, fractal branches, cyber grids, liquid rings, early-three-dimensional
glyphs
kaleidoscopic radial symmetry; `--radius-*` between 1rem and 2rem

## Typography
bulbous or early-3D display lettering for short names
paired with a crisp digital sans or mono that remains readable over moving color
Suggested: Rubik Glitch, Monoton, Modak, Syne, Unbounded

## Origins
late 1980s–mid-1990s; rave flyers, early computer graphics, and emerging
cyberculture
joined psychedelic color and imagery with the first broadly visible wave of
digital manipulation
fractals
rave graphics, and network mythology
the name became widely recognizable through the Cyberdelia club in the 1995
film Hackers
though the visual tendency predates that film
