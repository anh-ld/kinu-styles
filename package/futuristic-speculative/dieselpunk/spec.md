# Dieselpunk

## Signature
riveted metal, military stencils, soot textures, propaganda graphics, heavy
machinery
angular panels
`--noise: 1`, `--depth: 1`

## Suited for
games, entertainment, vehicles, events, alternate-history products, cinematic
campaigns

## Layout
heavy military-industrial panels and poster-like sections built around
machinery, maps,
and mission data
using hard hierarchy and compact command zones

## Colors
- primary: warning red or rust
- secondary: khaki or worn steel
- base-100 → base-300: steel gray, charcoal, soot black
- soot black, steel gray, khaki, rust, faded cream, warning red with oily
  highlights
  and low ambient brightness

## Imagery
riveted vehicles, factories, engines, uniforms, propaganda print, smoke,
searchlights
monumental interwar architecture

## Density
high functional density in instrument or mission areas
offset by large cinematic machinery crops and blunt declarative messages
`*-sm` size variants preserve this compact working density

## Motion
piston movement, stamped labels, heavy shutter wipes, vibrating engines,
searchlight sweeps
forceful mechanical state changes

## Shape
riveted angular plates, armored windows, stencil blocks, vents, gauges
thick structural braces with worn edges
`--radius-*` between 0 and 0.25rem

## Typography
condensed industrial sans-serif, military stencil, and tabular instrument type
reserving propaganda-style capitals for major commands or chapter titles
Suggested: Black Ops One, Oswald, Teko, Staatliches, Special Elite

## Origins
imagined source era from roughly World War I–the 1950s; term coined in 2001
tabletop game culture and retrofuturist fiction
game designer Lewis Pollak coined the term in 2001 for Children of the Sun
using it for speculative worlds built from interwar and mid-century diesel
technology
mass warfare, pulp illustration, radio, aviation, and industrial modernity
later communities broadened it into fashion and visual design
Sources: wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieselpunk
