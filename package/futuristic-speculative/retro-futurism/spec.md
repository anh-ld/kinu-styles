# Retro-Futurism

## Signature
vintage technology, space-age geometry, chrome accents, period typography,
analog controls
imagined futures
`--depth: 1`

## Suited for
entertainment, gaming, technology campaigns, exhibitions, experimental
product launches

## Layout
combine a period-inspired control grid with sweeping space-age vistas
arranging analog details around one optimistic vision of an imagined tomorrow

## Colors
- primary: period orange or teal
- secondary: avocado or chrome-blue
- base-100 → base-300: era-specific cream, warm gray, machine gray
- era cream, orange, teal, avocado, space black, and chrome
  with luminous accents that feel predicted by the past

## Imagery
vintage technology, speculative vehicles, rockets, domes, robots, analog
instruments
archival-style illustrations of future life

## Density
medium; museum-like explanatory detail balanced with broad cinematic scenes
and clearly separated control clusters

## Motion
analog dial movement, mechanical reveals, starfield travel
cinematic object entrances with deliberately period-flavored timing

## Shape
portholes, rocket fins, radar circles, capsule windows, antennae
streamlined control panels drawn from the chosen historical era; `--radius-*`
between 0.75rem
and 1.5rem

## Typography
period science-fiction display face for short titles
paired with a contemporary readable sans or mono that keeps the concept usable
Suggested: Orbitron, Michroma, Audiowide, Bruno Ace, Syncopate

## Origins
historical source material from the late 19th–20th centuries; named revival
from the 1980s
publishing
illustration, film, architecture, and product design; the modern label
developed in 1980s publishing
associated with Lloyd Dunn's Retrofuturism projects; unlike a simple vintage
revival
it foregrounds the tension between past expectations and the future that
actually arrived
Sources: academia.org.br (Retrofuturismo)
