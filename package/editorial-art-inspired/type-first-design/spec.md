# Type-First Design

## Signature
oversized headlines, expressive letterforms, extreme scale contrast, tight
cropping, minimal imagery
typographic composition

## Suited for
agencies, portfolios, launches, events, editorial brands, campaign websites

## Layout
oversized words establish the grid; crop letterforms at viewport edges;
navigation, media
and actions positioned in the negative spaces created by the type

## Colors
high-contrast fields; text color is the main compositional signal
secondary hues limited so letterform mass stays dominant
- primary: the headline's dominant signal color
- secondary: one controlled counter-color
- base-100, base-200, base-300: stark white-to-gray or black-to-charcoal
  progression

## Imagery
minimal, or embedded as a crop, mask, texture
or counterpoint within the typographic composition rather than given equal
billing

## Density
open with sparse monumental statements
supporting facts collected into compact editorial blocks that do not dilute
the typographic idea

## Motion
animate variable axes, line breaks, tracking, masks, and scale
preserve word recognition and settle before longer reading begins

## Shape
letterforms, baselines, underlines, and text boxes as primary shapes; buttons
and containers visually subordinate
`--radius-*` set to 0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
one unmistakable display family and one quiet reading face; test every
dramatic crop responsively
never sacrifice complete labels or body legibility
Suggested: Syne, Unbounded, Archivo Black, Bricolage Grotesque, Anton

## Origins
modernist precedents in the early 20th century; recurring in digital design
European modernist print design and later screen publishing; a current
practice label
not a formally founded movement
— lineage through modernist designers who made typography carry structure and
voice
especially Bauhaus and Swiss practitioners; web fonts
and responsive type systems let digital products use type as the primary
visual material
