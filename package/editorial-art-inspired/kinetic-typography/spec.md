# Kinetic Typography

## Signature
animated letterforms, variable font axes, rhythmic timing, text morphing,
scrolling type
scale transitions

## Suited for
music, entertainment, product launches, explainers, storytelling, motion-led
brand experiences

## Layout
sequence text across stable stage areas; keep navigation and body copy anchored
design each motion beat around one phrase the viewer can fully read

## Colors
limited high-contrast palette that keeps moving words legible; color changes
only to mark rhythm
speaker
emphasis, or narrative phase
- primary: the active-word color
- secondary: the alternate speaker or rhythm color
- base-100, base-200, base-300: still, high-contrast neutral steps

## Imagery
photography, video, or abstract footage responding to the text cadence as a
background layer
never obscuring the active phrase

## Density
each animated beat sparse — one short statement at a time
static transcripts or summaries available for detailed content

## Motion
drive weight, width, slant, scale, tracking, and position from speech or
musical rhythm
controlled entrances
readable holds, decisive exits

## Shape
frames, paths, and masks derived from baselines, glyph counters
and text trajectories so supporting graphics feel born from the words
`--radius-*` set to 0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
variable families with stable interpolation; preserve recognizable glyph
forms during motion
provide a complete static reduced-motion state
Suggested: Anybody, Roboto Flex, Recursive, Unbounded, Climate Crisis

## Origins
film-title precedents in the 1950s; digital expansion from the 1990s; film
title design
and motion graphics
Saul Bass's 1959 titles for Alfred Hitchcock's North by Northwest made
animated letterforms part
of the film's spatial drama; television, music video, and later browser and
app animation made
the method a routine digital design tool
