# Pop Art

## Signature
comic dots, commercial imagery, bold outlines, primary colors, repeated
motifs, oversized slogans

## Suited for
retail, entertainment, music, youth campaigns, editorial, energetic brand
activations

## Layout
compose like a commercial poster or comic spread; repeat products or
portraits in grids
break the pattern with one oversized slogan or crop

## Colors
bright primary and secondary inks with black outlines and white punch-outs
flat color swaps applied across repeated imagery
- primary: print red or cobalt
- secondary: yellow or cyan
- base-100: white
- base-200: off-white
- base-300: light cool gray
- texture: `--noise: 1`

## Imagery
consumer products, celebrity portraits, comic fragments, advertising symbols
and silkscreened photographs with bold contour and repetition

## Density
high visual impact with low to medium copy; repeat short phrases
and images instead of filling the page with explanation

## Motion
cycle colorways, repeat tiles, smash-zoom focal objects,
and pop speech-like messages into place with abrupt graphic timing

## Shape
thick outlines, circles, product silhouettes, comic panels, target motifs
and hard rectangular crops at exaggerated scale; `--radius-*` set to 0 or
0.25rem

## Typography
oversized heavy sans-serif or comic display type in short slogans; plain
compact face for prices
dates
and functional details
Suggested: Bangers, Bowlby One SC, Anton, Luckiest Guy, Alfa Slab One

## Origins
mid-1950s–1960s; Britain, then the United States
began with British artists examining mass media and consumer culture in the
1950s
then became a major American movement in the 1960s; artists appropriated
comics, advertising
television, celebrity, packaging, and commercial reproduction methods; flat
color, repetition
and enlarged print marks became an enduring source for interface graphics
