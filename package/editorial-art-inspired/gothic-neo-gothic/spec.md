# Gothic / Neo-Gothic

## Signature
blackletter type, pointed arches, tracery, dark palette, ornamental borders,
medieval symbols

## Suited for
fashion, music, games, entertainment, literature, dark luxury branding

## Layout
tall axial compositions, pointed framing, and cathedral-like vertical rhythm
content placed within portals
niches, and ornamental border systems

## Colors
black, midnight blue, oxblood, parchment,
and cold metallic accents with small jewel tones that resemble stained glass
- primary: oxblood or a deep stained-glass jewel tone
- secondary: cold silver or midnight blue
- base-100, base-200, base-300: dark charcoal, midnight, black

## Imagery
Gothic architecture, dramatic portraiture, tracery, sculpture, reliquaries,
medieval symbols
and dark material studies with directional light

## Density
medium density; ornament concentrated around entrances and headings; reading
areas dark, calm
and spacious

## Motion
candle-like light shifts, slow vertical reveals, opening portals, tracery draws
restrained atmospheric haze

## Shape
pointed arches, quatrefoils, shields, spires, lancet frames, tracery
and narrow vertical panels with sharp ornamental detail; `--radius-*` set to
0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
blackletter or dramatic display serif only for short titles
highly readable old-style serif or sans-serif for navigation and body copy
Suggested: UnifrakturCook, UnifrakturMaguntia, Grenze Gotisch, Pirata One,
Cinzel Decorative

## Origins
medieval Gothic from the 12th–16th centuries; Gothic Revival from the late
18th–19th centuries
medieval Europe
revival especially influential in Britain; Gothic architecture developed
through pointed arches
rib vaults
tracery, vertical structure, and stained glass
the Revival reinterpreting medieval form for churches
institutions, interiors, print, and nationalist narratives
current UI often combines both periods with later gothic subculture
Sources: riba.org (Gothic Revival),
vam.ac.uk/articles/stained-glass-gothic-revival-and-beyond
