# Heroic Realism

## Signature
idealized workers and citizens, upward gazes, low-angle monumentality,
banners and sun rays
industrial horizons
declarative slogans

## Suited for
museums, historical interpretation, civic storytelling, sports, industrial
heritage
knowingly monumental campaigns

## Layout
triangular or processional composition around one commanding figure or
unified group
low viewpoint with clear forward or upward movement toward the central message

## Colors
banner red, warm gold, cream, cobalt, earth, and steel tones in broad modeled
areas
radiant light reserved for the promised goal or collective horizon
- primary: banner red or cobalt
- secondary: warm gold
- base-100, base-200, base-300: cream, pale earth, muted steel-gray

## Imagery
painterly realist scenes of workers, farmers, soldiers, athletes, scientists
or citizens idealized against industry, agriculture, construction, or space
retain historical context when quoting propaganda

## Density
copy low and rhetorical; one concise slogan paired with a monumental image;
dates, sources
or interpretation moved into a plainly separated information region

## Motion
slow banner unfurls, marching parallax, rising light, sequential crowd reveals
weighty forward motion
— never playful or decorative

## Shape
ascending triangles, radiating suns, broad banners, monumental plinths,
strong diagonals
simplified industrial silhouettes that reinforce collective scale;
`--radius-*` set to 0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
short statements in heavy condensed capitals or a forceful slab;
uncompromised legibility
avoid imitating a specific political script or insignia without relevant context
Suggested: Oswald, Roboto Slab, Archivo Black, Teko, Bebas Neue

## Origins
especially prominent in state art of the 1930s–1950s; multiple national
propaganda systems
including the Soviet Union and fascist Europe; broad
politically charged label for idealized legible depictions of workers,
soldiers, leaders,
and national bodies — overlaps Soviet Socialist Realism and fascist visual
propaganda
so not a neutral unified school; current design borrows monumental scale, low
viewpoints, rays
and declarative type while separating those devices from the original ideology
Sources: moma.org/collection/terms/social-realism,
