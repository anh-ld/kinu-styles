# Bauhaus

## Signature
primary colors, circles and rectangles, functional geometry, asymmetric balance
sans-serif typography
visible structure

## Suited for
architecture, cultural institutions, portfolios, education, design-focused
products

## Layout
organize content through functional asymmetric balance; circles, bars, images
and text visibly participate in one rational spatial construction

## Colors
red, yellow, and blue as purposeful primary signals against black, white, and
warm paper neutrals
— never random decorative confetti
- primary: Bauhaus red
- secondary: cobalt blue
- base-100: warm paper
- base-200: off-white
- base-300: light neutral gray

## Imagery
architectural photography, photograms, workshop objects, diagrams,
and geometric illustration that reveal material
process, or structure

## Density
medium density with clearly bounded teaching or information groups
enough open field for each geometric relationship to register

## Motion
translate, rotate, and reconfigure geometric parts to explain function or
sequence
direct mechanical timing without ornamental easing

## Shape
build from the circle, square, triangle, bar, and line
each form given a repeated compositional or semantic purpose; `--radius-*`
set to 0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
geometric sans-serif with strong scale contrast; short declarative headings
disciplined alignment that integrates type into the construction
Suggested: Josefin Sans, Jost, League Spartan, Raleway, Montserrat

## Origins
1919–1933; Weimar, Dessau, and Berlin, Germany
Walter Gropius founded the school in Weimar in 1919 seeking to reconnect art,
craft
architecture, and industrial production; moved to Dessau
and later Berlin before closing under Nazi pressure in 1933; teachers
and students dispersed internationally, spreading its functional typography
geometric experiment, and workshop model through modern design education
Sources: moma.org/collection/terms/bauhaus
