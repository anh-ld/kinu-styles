# Art Deco

## Signature
stepped geometry, fan motifs, metallic accents, symmetrical composition, high
contrast
geometric display type

## Suited for
hospitality, luxury products, entertainment, events, premium branding

## Layout
symmetrical axial compositions with stepped tiers, centered entrances
and mirrored decorative structures leading toward a glamorous focal point

## Colors
black or deep jewel tones with ivory and controlled gold, silver, or brass
accents
crisp high-contrast separation
- primary: controlled gold or brass
- secondary: emerald, sapphire, or oxblood
- base-100, base-200, base-300: deep charcoal, ink black, jewel-tinted black

## Imagery
dramatic architecture, polished products, theatrical portraiture, fan motifs
and geometric city or stage imagery with hard directional light

## Density
low to medium; metallic linework and monumental headings frame a concise
premium message

## Motion
reveal fan patterns radially; move through stepped masks
brief metallic glints or curtain-like openings with measured theatrical timing

## Shape
sunbursts, fans, chevrons, stepped pyramids, fluted lines, octagons
and symmetrical frames with sharp geometric precision; `--radius-*` set to 0
or 0.25rem

## Typography
geometric display capitals with generous tracking for short headings
restrained serif or sans-serif for details and controls
Suggested: Limelight, Poiret One, Cinzel, Josefin Sans, Italiana

## Origins
1920s–1930s; Paris, then international; developed from prewar French luxury
design
defining international showcase at the 1925 Exposition internationale des
arts décoratifs et
industriels modernes in Paris; combined craft and expensive materials with
stepped geometry,
streamlined machinery, and imagery drawn from many cultures; spread through
architecture,
transport, fashion, cinema, and graphic design
