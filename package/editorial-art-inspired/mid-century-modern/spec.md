# Mid-Century Modern

## Signature
warm muted palette, simple geometry, humanist illustration, tapered forms,
clean layouts
optimistic motifs

## Suited for
architecture, furniture, lifestyle, editorial, hospitality, design-led commerce

## Layout
functional modular blocks with relaxed asymmetry
clean product grids mixed with open illustrated or photographic living spaces

## Colors
mustard, olive, burnt orange, teal, walnut,
and warm cream in muted arrangements that feel optimistic without becoming
childish
- primary: burnt orange or teal
- secondary: mustard or olive
- base-100: warm cream
- base-200: pale tan
- base-300: muted walnut-beige

## Imagery
furniture, architecture, domestic objects, and humanist illustrations with
simple silhouettes
warm materials
and period-appropriate styling

## Density
medium density; practical information presented clearly while signature objects
and color fields get room to breathe

## Motion
simple sliding panels, gentle rotating motifs
straightforward object reveals with the unfussy confidence of period motion
graphics

## Shape
tapered legs, boomerangs, rounded rectangles, lozenges, starbursts
and simple circles balanced against clean structural lines; `--radius-*`
between 0.25rem and 1rem

## Typography
friendly humanist sans-serif paired with a restrained slab or serif;
confident headings
and comfortable body proportions
Suggested: Josefin Sans, Libre Franklin, Fira Sans, League Spartan, Bitter

## Origins
roughly mid-1940s–late 1960s; postwar Europe and the United States
with influential Nordic and Californian centers; grouped postwar
architecture, furniture, products
and graphics emphasizing functional planning, new industrial materials, and
clean organic geometry
diverse rather than a single organization
— the label was popularized retrospectively by Cara Greenberg's 1984 book
Mid-Century Modern
rather than being the universal name used by its original designers
Sources: designmuseum.dk/en/exhibition/danish-modern,
