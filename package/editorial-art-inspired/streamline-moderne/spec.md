# Streamline Moderne

## Signature
aerodynamic curves, horizontal speed lines, rounded corners, chrome accents,
ocean-liner forms
machine-age simplicity

## Suited for
travel, automotive, hospitality, appliances, retro technology,
transport-inspired brands

## Layout
long horizontal bands, rounded leading edges,
and streamlined section flow that makes the interface feel like a vessel
moving forward

## Colors
cream, navy, sea green, signal red, and polished chrome; restrained gradients
suggesting enamel
speed, and machinery
- primary: navy or sea green
- secondary: signal red
- base-100: cream
- base-200: pale silver
- base-300: cool light gray

## Imagery
ocean liners, trains, automobiles, terminals, appliances,
and aerodynamic industrial details photographed or illustrated from low
sweeping angles

## Density
medium density in long horizontal groups; vertical clutter low; prioritize
routes, features
or destinations

## Motion
glide panels laterally; trail restrained speed lines
sweep highlights along curved surfaces with smooth constant acceleration

## Shape
aerodynamic capsules, horizontal ribs, portholes, rounded corners, teardrops
and chrome bands stripped of ornate Deco detail; `--radius-*` between 0.75rem
and 1.5rem

## Typography
streamlined geometric or condensed sans-serif with broad horizontal spacing
labels compact and aligned to the motion bands
Suggested: Righteous, Audiowide, Josefin Sans, Barlow Condensed, Poiret One

## Origins
1930s–early 1940s; industrial design and architecture in the United States
and Europe
the machine-age
aerodynamic branch of late Art Deco; designers translated aircraft, ship, train
and wind-tunnel research and imagery into rounded corners, horizontal bands,
smooth shells,
and chrome details
— even for stationary products
reached a mass audience at fairs such as the 1939–1940 New York World's Fair
