# UrBling

## Signature
silhouetted city infrastructure, graffiti and stencil lettering, paint
splatters and drips
wings and crests
flames and tribal motifs, sepia-red urban palettes; `--noise: 1`

## Suited for
music, streetwear, skate culture, sports, automotive products, events, gaming
mid-2000s nostalgic campaigns

## Layout
layer a silhouetted skyline, road, or power-line horizon behind a dense
central crest, product
or performer
then pin practical navigation to one stable poster-like column

## Colors
asphalt black, concrete gray, sepia, rust, orange, yellow,
and deep red with dirty white or silver highlights and visibly weathered
transitions
- primary: orange or deep red
- secondary: rust or warning yellow
- base-100/200/300: concrete gray, charcoal, asphalt black
- texture: `--noise: 1`

## Imagery
streetlights, power lines, overpasses, buildings, graffiti walls, skate
scenes, custom cars
speakers, hoodies
urban night photography treated with splatter and grit

## Density
high graphic density with short promotional language; schedules, prices,
track lists
and actions kept on solid high-contrast fields

## Motion
stamp stencils, spray or wipe paint, drip selected marks, shift layered skylines
reveal crests with gritty mechanical timing rather than gemstone sparkle

## Shape
heraldic shields, wings, flames, tribal curls, splatters, drips, torn edges,
stencil blocks
sharp custom-lettered emblems; `--radius-*` 0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
mix slab sans-serif, graffiti, stencil, and gothic display faces for names or
slogans
grounding longer copy in a sturdy condensed sans-serif
Suggested: Black Ops One, Rock Salt, Special Elite, Roboto Slab, Oswald

## Origins
early–late 2000s; commercial youth branding shaped by hip-hop, streetwear,
graffiti,
and club imagery
retrospective classification for glossy commercial "urban" graphics
— CARI catalogs the field as Urban Grunge and credits Froyo Tam with the
research label
much source imagery was corporate appropriation of Black and Latino cultural
forms
so its history is also a history of marketing, sanitization, and unequal
attribution
Sources: cari.institute/aesthetics/urban-grunge, cari.institute/history
