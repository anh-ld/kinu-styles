# Y2K

## Signature
chrome gradients, translucent plastic, bubble lettering, lens flares,
compact-device motifs
glossy icons; `--depth: 1`

## Suited for
fashion, beauty, music, social products, youth brands

## Layout
layered turn-of-the-millennium desktop composition: compact device-like windows
floating bubbles, product cutouts
small interface chrome

## Colors
silver, ice blue, hot pink, lime, white,
and translucent plastic tones with metallic gradients and sharp glossy
highlights
- primary: ice blue or hot pink
- secondary: lime or cool silver
- base-100/200/300: glossy white, pale ice, silver gray
- depth: `--depth: 1`

## Imagery
flip phones, compact cameras, CDs, lip gloss, chrome objects, translucent
electronics, lens flares
optimistic early digital imagery

## Density
medium with many small collectible details; major actions stay large
chrome ornament kept clear of labels

## Motion
loading shines, bubble floats, window pops, lens-flare sweeps
springy device interactions with brisk glossy timing

## Shape
bubbles, capsules, chrome stars, rounded device shells, translucent tabs,
compact beveled windows
`--radius-*` between 1rem and 2rem

## Typography
pair bubble or techno display lettering with a compact clean sans or pixel
accent
keeping body text crisp against metallic and translucent surfaces
Suggested: Audiowide, Michroma, Righteous, Syncopate, Zen Dots

## Origins
mid-1990s–around 2001 as a source style; major revival from the late 2010s;
global fashion, music
technology advertising, clubs, film, and graphic design
formed around the approach of the year 2000 and the mix of technological
optimism
and anxiety surrounding the millennium bug — chrome, translucent plastic,
wraparound forms
cyber imagery, late-1990s club culture
"Y2K" later broadened into a looser label for early-2000s fashion
so contemporary usage is not always historically precise
Sources: cari.institute/aesthetics/y2k-aesthetic, cari.institute/history
