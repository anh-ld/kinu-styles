# Hand-Drawn UI

## Signature
sketchy outlines, handwritten labels, imperfect icons, paper texture, playful
arrows
visible human variation
`--noise: 1;`

## Suited for
education, journaling, creative tools, children's products, personal brands

## Layout
loose sketchbook grid with playful annotations and arrows; forms, navigation
and repeated controls aligned consistently enough to use quickly

## Colors
- primary: clearest marker or crayon hue
- secondary: complementary watercolor tone
- base-100: off-white paper
- base-200: warm sketch paper
- base-300: lightly smudged gray paper
- texture: `--noise: 1;`

## Imagery
line drawings, annotated diagrams, imperfect icons, scanned marks sharing one
hand, tool,
and stroke character

## Density
moderate; breathing room around strokes; annotations never cross labels or
interactive targets

## Motion
draw lines on, tiny idle wobble to illustrations
erase or redraw state changes with brief imperfect timing

## Shape
uneven outlines, hand-drawn boxes, circles, arrows, underlines
paper edges with deliberate human variance
`--radius-*` between 0.25rem and 1rem

## Typography
handwritten type for headings and callouts; clean body face for longer reading
avoid shrinking irregular letters below comfortable sizes
Suggested: Patrick Hand, Architects Daughter, Kalam, Caveat, Gloria Hallelujah

## Origins
craft-revival precedents in the late 19th century; digital use throughout the
web era
Origin: illustration, lettering, and craft traditions adapted to screens
A technique category, not a dated movement; its claim
to visible human authorship echoes the Arts
and Crafts reaction against industrial standardization in late-19th-century
Britain; scanners
tablets, and vector tools later made imperfect lines practical components of
digital products
