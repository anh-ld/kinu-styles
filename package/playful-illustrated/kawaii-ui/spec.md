# Kawaii UI

## Signature
rounded characters, pastel colors, sparkling accents, expressive faces, tiny
decorative motifs
pill-shaped controls

## Suited for
games, social apps, stationery, children's products, playful lifestyle brands

## Layout
centered stacked flows, collectible item clusters, friendly framed scenes
essential actions large and free from surrounding decoration

## Colors
- primary: candy pink or lavender
- secondary: mint or sky blue
- base-100: cream
- base-200: pale blush
- base-300: slightly deeper pastel tint
- pastel base with candy-bright accents; dark cocoa or navy outlines
  tiny sparkle colors that never replace readable state contrast

## Imagery
chibi characters, expressive objects, food or animal motifs
tiny decorative faces with consistent rounded proportions

## Density
low to medium; small decorative details concentrated at edges; clean space
around text and controls
use `*-lg` daisyUI size variants to reinforce the chunky, informal scale

## Motion
blinks, tiny hops, squashy button presses, sparkle bursts, cheerful success
reactions with quick
light timing

## Shape
circles, hearts, stars, clouds, rounded pills, scalloped frames
soft character silhouettes with thick clean outlines; `--radius-*` between
1rem and 2rem

## Typography
rounded display lettering for short cheerful headings; simple readable body face
avoid cramming complex scripts into tiny badges
Suggested: M PLUS Rounded 1c, Zen Maru Gothic, Mochiy Pop One, DynaPuff, Coiny

## Origins
modern commercial form from the 1970s; global digital expansion from the 1990s
Origin: Japan
"Kawaii" means cute; the modern commercial culture around it took
recognizable form in 1970s
Japan through youthful handwriting, character goods, and changing consumer
culture; Sanrio
introduced Hello Kitty in 1974; character branding, games, emoji, stickers,
and mobile culture
subsequently carried kawaii visual language worldwide
