# Superflat Pop

## Signature
flat perspective, candy colors, dense cartoon motifs, polished outlines,
repeated characters
Japanese pop energy

## Suited for
fashion, art, entertainment, games, collectibles, playful commerce

## Layout
flat panoramic field where characters, motifs, products, and patterns share
one shallow plane
repetition and scale direct attention

## Colors
- bright candy pink, red, yellow, cyan, green, and purple with clean black or
  colored outlines
  little atmospheric shading
- primary: candy pink or red
- secondary: cyan or yellow
- base-100: clean white
- base-200: pale candy tint
- base-300: light cool gray

## Imagery
polished cartoon characters, flowers, eyes, icons, consumer objects,
anime-influenced motifs
repeated decorative figures

## Density
high visual density with medium copy; simple flat color pockets reserved for
product details
prices, and controls

## Motion
pan across flat scenes, tile motifs, pop characters into place
swap expressions or colors with crisp two-dimensional timing

## Shape
flat contours, rounded character silhouettes, flowers, eyes, stars, bubbles
repeated emblematic forms without perspective depth; `--radius-*` between
0.75rem and 1.5rem

## Typography
bold rounded Japanese-pop display type for short headings; highly legible
Gothic or sans-serif body
carefully set multilingual text
Suggested: Mochiy Pop One, M PLUS Rounded 1c, Dela Gothic One, Kosugi Maru,
Noto Sans JP

## Origins
Superflat articulated in 2000; commercial-pop expansion through the
2000s–early 2010s; Japan
Takashi Murakami's 2000 manifesto and exhibition used visual flatness
to connect Japanese art history, manga
anime, consumer culture, and the collapse of "high" and "low" categories
Superflat Pop names the especially commercial, character-driven expansion
through fashion, animation
motion graphics, and products; should remain connected to Murakami's critical
theory
not reduced to colorful cartoon flatness
Sources: mfa.org/news/takashi-murakami, cari.institute/aesthetics/superflat-pop
