# Collage

## Signature
cutout photography, torn edges, overlapping fragments, mixed media, scale
contrast, pasted textures
`--noise: 1;` and `--depth: 1;`

## Suited for
editorial, music, fashion, culture, portfolios, campaign storytelling

## Layout
layer fragments around a clear focal cutout; vary scale aggressively
preserve stable reading anchors beneath deliberate overlaps and off-grid
placement

## Colors
- small palette pulled from the source photographs, unified by repeated paper
  tones, ink colors
  or one overlay wash
- primary: recurring ink or overlay-wash hue
- secondary: strong photo-derived color
- base-100: clean paper
- base-200: aged paper
- base-300: shadowed paper

## Imagery
mix cutout photography, scans, handwriting, found print, paint
and torn paper while preserving visible edges and source texture

## Density
high visual density near focal compositions; concise copy
uncluttered paper space around important actions

## Motion
pasted entrances, small parallax offsets, torn-mask reveals
stop-frame rearrangements that retain a handmade feel

## Shape
torn contours, taped rectangles, rough circles, irregular masks
cropped fragments rather than polished component silhouettes; `--radius-*`
set to 0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
at most two contrasting display voices with one dependable reading face;
found type for accents only
never for essential instructions
Suggested: Archivo Black, Cormorant Garamond, Permanent Marker, Cutive Mono,
Syne

## Origins
named as a modern-art method around 1912; Paris, in Cubist work by Pablo Picasso
and Georges Braque; Dada
Surrealism, punk, editorial design, and digital image tools repeatedly
extended the technique
Sources: nationalgalleries.org/art-and-artists/glossary-terms/collage
