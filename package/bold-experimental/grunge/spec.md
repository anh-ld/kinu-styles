# Grunge

## Signature
distressed typography, scratches and stains, dirty textures, muted colors,
irregular layering
worn edges; `--noise: 1;`

## Suited for
music, gaming, streetwear, sports, entertainment, rebellious portfolios

## Layout
layer worn panels and irregular media around a stable central column or grid
damage erodes surfaces without destroying information order

## Colors
- dirty charcoal, rust, olive, burgundy, faded denim,
  and nicotine cream with low-chroma highlights and occasional raw warning color
- primary: rust or burgundy
- secondary: faded denim or olive
- base-100: nicotine cream
- base-200: stained beige
- base-300: muddy taupe

## Imagery
scratched photography, distressed posters, concrete, metal, dust, stains
weathered fabric with believable material wear

## Density
medium to high; texture around groups while protecting text interiors from
excessive noise
and contrast loss

## Motion
brief film flicker, scratch passes, rough judder, dust, and torn reveals used
sparingly
worn rather than broken

## Shape
chipped rectangles, rough masks, uneven borders, stains, tape
worn edges with little clean geometric perfection
`--radius-*` set to 0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
distressed, eroded, or metal-inspired type reserved for large headings
sturdy condensed sans or serif for all detailed reading
Suggested: Rubik Dirt, Special Elite, Black Ops One, Metal Mania, Pirata One

## Origins
late 1980s–1990s; alternative music culture and experimental editorial design
in the United States
not confined to Seattle or album packaging; distressed type, layered scans,
dirt, fragmentation
and unstable layouts entered mainstream graphic design in the 1990s
David Carson's art direction of Ray Gun 1992–1995 a prominent reference for
expressive
rule-breaking editorial typography
Sources: thegraphicdesignschool.com/design-history/david-carson/
