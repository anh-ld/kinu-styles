# Brutalism

## Signature
raw HTML-like structure, default-style controls, stark contrast, exposed grid
oversized typography, minimal polish

## Suited for
editorial sites, artist portfolios, cultural projects, countercultural brands

## Layout
expose the document flow, grid, and navigation directly; oversized content
blocks
abrupt scale changes
unapologetic full-width sections

## Colors
- black and white with one harsh utility accent; preserve default-like link
  and focus colors when they strengthen the raw interface logic
- primary: harsh utility blue or red
- secondary: black
- base-100: white
- base-200: light gray
- base-300: visibly darker structural gray

## Imagery
unpolished photographs, scans, screenshots, direct media embeds with honest
aspect ratios
visible captions
minimal retouching

## Density
either stark sparseness or dense document-like lists
make the information structure explicit rather than softening it into cards

## Motion
immediate state changes, hard cuts, native scrolling, occasional raw marquees
avoid smooth decorative transitions that imply polish

## Shape
sharp rectangles, thick rules, native controls, exposed borders
full-bleed media with no ornamental radius system
`--radius-*` set to 0 or 0.25rem

## Typography
system sans, grotesque, serif, or monospace faces at blunt scale contrasts
preserve underlined links and obvious semantic heading levels
Suggested: Archivo Black, Space Mono, IBM Plex Mono, Work Sans, Courier Prime

## Origins
1950s–1970s in architecture; digital reinterpretation from the 2010s; postwar
Britain and Europe
with international development; Alison Smithson used "New Brutalism" in 1953
Le Corbusier's raw concrete an important precedent
web designers borrowed the name for sites that expose structure or refuse
polished convention
but that digital usage is an analogy rather than a direct architectural method
